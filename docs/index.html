The drift diffusion model

Noisy evidence accumulates until it reaches a boundary. Drift: how fast evidence accumulates. Boundary: how much evidence you require. (Ratcliff 1978; Ratcliff and McKoon 2008)

Drift diffusion: one task, three levels

Simulated. Computational: which way? Algorithmic: accumulate to a bound (Ratcliff 1978). Implementation: contested: ramps or steps (Shadlen and Newsome 2001; Latimer et al. 2015; Steinemann et al. 2024).

Caution varies, within and between us

Binocular rivalry

Each eye gets a different image, and what you see alternates while the input stays the same. Report: what you say you saw. Performance: what you can do with it. (Blake and Logothetis 2002)

The same input, a different percept

Live with glasses. This GIF is simulated (Blake and Logothetis 2002). Report vs performance, subject vs object (Nagel 1974; Chalmers 1995).

Continuous flash suppression

A changing mask in one eye hides a steady image in the other, until it breaks through. (Tsuchiya and Koch 2005; Stein, Hebart, and Sterzer 2011)

Blended

Seen or not, while the screen stays the same

Schematic: click to play. Live needs glasses (Tsuchiya and Koch 2005; Zhu, Drewes, and Melcher 2016). What it proves about the unconscious is debated (Moors et al. 2017).
