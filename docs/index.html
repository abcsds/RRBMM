Talking about the mind through its methods

Alberto Barradas (Xiutik)

2026-11-05

You only meet a mind through a method

A construct is a latent variable. A method is its encoder. Tonight, you are the dataset.

Last time: encoders infer what they cannot see

  • Variational autoencoders (Kingma and Welling 2013)
  • Representation learning (Bengio, Courville, and Vincent 2013)
  • Embedded spaces: distance as meaning

Same move tonight: nobody observes “attention”. We infer it from what people do.

The map, in four cuts

Levels of analysis. What is computed, how, and in what (Marr 1982).

Symbolic vs connectionist. Rules over symbols, or learned vectors (Fodor and Pylyshyn 1988; Rumelhart and McClelland 1986; Lake and Baroni 2023).

Representing vs performing. Knowing that, knowing how. A different axis from what signal trains you (Squire 2004; Doya 2000).

Hardware and software. A good start. It breaks at memory, metabolism and the body (Richards and Lillicrap 2022; Sennesh et al. 2022).

ACT-R: symbolic rules over learned activations, turning knowing-that into knowing-how (Anderson et al. 2019). Tonight we skip learning and development.

Constructive: it exists once you can build it

Constructive mathematics

To claim it exists, construct it. A proof is a program (Bishop 1967; Wadler 2015).

Cognitive science

To claim a construct, give the procedure that measures it (Cronbach and Meehl 1955; Borsboom, Mellenbergh, and Heerden 2004).

A parallel, not a lineage.

Aggregates: reflective, a latent cause behind the indicators; or formative, a sum of its parts.

Two measures, one word

  • Tasks and surveys of “self-regulation” barely agree (Eisenberg et al. 2019)
  • One name, many constructs; many names, one construct (Wulff and Mata 2025)
  • Cognitive dissonance: the classic design failed across 39 labs, N = 4,898 (Vaidis et al. 2024)

Every word from here on comes with its method.

You do not just see the world

Priming: a prior changes what you see

Live. Mooney 1957 (Mooney 1957). Word for the glossary: prior (Knill and Pouget 2004).

Change blindness: looking is not seeing

Live. Flashes about 3 times a second: click to play. (Rensink, O’Regan, and Clark 1997)

From the body to wanting

Interoception: the system runs its own body

  • Heartbeat counting, 30 seconds, hands off your pulse
  • Then: how sure are you? Accuracy and confidence are different measures (Garfinkel et al. 2015)
  • A contested task, which is the lesson (Desmedt, Luminet, and Corneille 2018)
  • Affect as a construction from body signals and concepts (Barrett 2017)

Monotropism: one thing at a time, deeply

Theory from autistic lived experience, measurement only starting (Murray, Lesser, and Lawson 2005; Garau et al. 2023; Dwyer et al. 2025). Ask, do not infer.

Simulated. Computational: which way? Algorithmic: accumulate to a bound (Ratcliff 1978). Implementation: contested: ramps or steps (Shadlen and Newsome 2001; Latimer et al. 2015; Steinemann et al. 2024).

Simulated. A parameter that varies, not a personality type.

Attention

Binding: where is the red T?

Live. Features pop out, conjunctions take attention (A. M. Treisman and Gelade 1980; Roskies 1999). Vision language models show binding failures too (Campbell et al. 2024).

N-back: feel the load

Live. Raise a hand on a match (Kirchner 1958; Owen et al. 2005). That is all the memory we do tonight.

Consciousness

The same input, a different percept

Live with glasses. This GIF is simulated (Blake and Logothetis 2002). Report vs performance, subject vs object (Nagel 1974; Chalmers 1995).

Seen or not, while the screen stays the same

Schematic: click to play. Live needs glasses (Tsuchiya and Koch 2005; Zhu, Drewes, and Melcher 2016). What it proves about the unconscious is debated (Moors et al. 2017).

Rival theories, one pre-agreed test

  • Theories of consciousness differ in which set of things they count as conscious (Seth and Bayne 2022)
  • Adversarial collaboration: agree on the prediction before the data (Cogitate Consortium et al. 2025)
  • The results challenged key tenets of both theories. Then over 100 researchers called one of them untestable (IIT-Concerned et al. 2025)
  • Could this be conscious? (Butlin et al. 2023; Seth 2026)

What to do with it

Where was your mind just now?

  • Hands up: on the talk, or elsewhere?
  • That is experience sampling: thought probes, and you just ran one on yourself
  • Default mode: an active network, not idling (Smallwood et al. 2021; Menon 2023)
  • People avoid effort (Kool et al. 2010; David, Vassena, and Bijleveld 2024). Is mind wandering the path of least resistance? A question, not a finding

Attention is what we trade

Yours. Manage it. Around 30 minutes outside helps a little, on average (Bell et al. 2025). Meditation: modest effects, real adverse ones (Goyal et al. 2014; Farias et al. 2020).

Theirs. A commodity: cognitive autonomy as a right worth naming (Simon 1971; Farahany 2023). Plain GPT-4: grades up 48 % with it, down 17 % once it is gone (Bastani et al. 2025).

Ours. A social resource. Ask, do not infer. Mixed autistic and non-autistic chains lose detail faster than either alone (Milton 2012; Crompton et al. 2020).

The border

Past here: culture and anthropology

It feels like Plato’s realm. It behaves like World 3: made by minds, and real for all of us (Popper 1972; Searle 1995).

  • Language lets us share it (Tomasello et al. 2005)
  • Isomorphisms: we can now compare the geometry of a model and a brain (Zada et al. 2024)
  • Intersubjective is where we stop, on purpose
